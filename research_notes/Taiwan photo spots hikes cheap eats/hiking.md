## 2. Difficulty, distance, duration, elevation

### Takeaway
Only partial stats were confirmed. Taipei-area hikes are short (30 min–3 h) but some, such as Huangdidian and Xiaozishan, involve exposed rock scrambles with chains and ropes. The high-mountain routes (Xueshan, Yushan, Dabajian, Qilai, Nanhu, Jiaming Lake) are multi-day trips at 3,000 m+.

### Cited Findings
- **Elephant Mountain**: easy–moderate; 20–30 min to main viewpoint; round trip 1.5–2 h — [Taipei Tourism Guide / search summary](https://www.taipeitourism.org/elephant-mountain-taipei-hike/)
- **Qixingshan**: Xiaoyoukeng → Qixingshan peak → Lengshuikeng traverse 2–3 h total; summit 1,120 m — [Taiwan Obsessed: Taipei to Yangmingshan](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/taipei-to-yangmingshan/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixing_Mountain_(Taipei))
- **Jinmianshan**: 30–40 min to the top; trailhead 15 min walk from MRT — [Medium/brownbeat](https://brownbeat.medium.com/taiwan-travels-jinmianshan-hiking-trail-a7d059ced638)
- **Sandiaoling Waterfall Trail**: moderate, 3.9 mi (~6.3 km), 2–2.5 h; three waterfalls; first part follows the Pingxi railway line — [AllTrails Ruifang waterfalls](https://www.alltrails.com/taiwan/new-taipei-city/ruifang/waterfall)
- **Huangdidian**: narrow exposed rock spine with sheer drops, using iron rungs, chains and ropes; 360° summit views — [Foreigners in Taiwan](https://www.foreignersintaiwan.com/blog/huangdidian-trail) / [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/taiwan/new-taipei-city/palace-of-the-emperor-huangdidian-trail)
- **Xiaozishan / Pingxi Crags**: steep rock-faced peaks, carved steps with ropes; round trip 2–3 h — [AllTrails Xiaozishan](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/taiwan/new-taipei-city/xiaozishan-trail); [isp.tw](https://www.isp.tw/pingxi-crags-xiaozi-mountain-xiaozishan-trail/)
- **Holy Mother / Shengmu Trail (Matcha Mtn)**: steep; 6 km one way / 12 km round trip; 5–6 h; Sanjiaolun Mtn 1,028 m — [AllTrails Our Lady Trail](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/taiwan/yilan/our-lady-trail); [unlockingtaiwan](https://unlockingtaiwan.com/matcha-mountain-yilan/)
- **Maolan Trail (Sun Moon Lake)**: 3 km, from beside Mingtan Junior High School to the Yuchih branch of the Tea Research and Extension Station; tea fields and lake panorama; open 09:00–17:00 — [Sun Moon Lake NSA](https://www.sunmoonlake.gov.tw/en/attractions/detail/147)
- **Xueshan**: 3,886 m; 369 Cabin at the 6.9 km mark of the trail — [Wikipedia Xueshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xueshan); [unlockingtaiwan Xueshan guide](https://unlockingtaiwan.com/xueshan-hiking-guide/)
- **Yushan**: 3,952 m, the highest peak in Taiwan / NE Asia. Standard plans are 2D1N (official) or a safer 3D2N — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_Shan); [wilds.com.tw](https://www.wilds.com.tw/blog/yushan-hiking-priority-permit-foreigners)
- **Hehuanshan main**: 3,422 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hehuanshan)
- **Walami Trail**: eastern section of the Batongguan Traversing Trail. 13.6 km from the trailhead via Shanfeng, Jiaxin and Huangma to Walami Cabin. Without a permit you can go to Shanfeng Waterfall (1.7 km) and on to Jiaxin campground (4.5 km) — [Yushan NP / search summary](https://www.ysnp.gov.tw/En/RecreationArea/e7d3a77b-854f-4fc0-aeb0-24278b0a9678?Tab=4&ID=c0e30500-8374-4eaf-b624-0f8b03166798); [Taiwan Hikes Walami](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/walami-trail.html)
- **Qilai Nanhua**: a typhoon destroyed the original trailhead, and the new trailhead adds 3 km each way, so overall difficulty went up — [braveontw / search summary](https://www.braveontw.com/post/taiwan-hiking-permits-foreigners)
- **Dabajianshan**: climbing to the very top of the Daba spire is no longer allowed (sacred to indigenous peoples, and dangerous); hikers stop at its base — [Taiwan Hikes Dabajian](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/100-peaks-dabajian-mountain.html); [Taiwan Hiking Journal Aug 2026](https://taiwanhikingjournal.blogspot.com/2026/08/dabajianshan-daba-peaks-trail.html)

### Inferences
- Suggested app difficulty tags: Easy (Elephant Mtn, Maolan, Shimenshan, Kenting forest, Dali-Datong partial, Taroko Terrace), Moderate (Qixingshan, Jinmianshan, Teapot Mtn, Bitou Cape, Sandiaoling, Caoling, Hehuan main/east, Walami to Jiaxin), Hard/exposed (Huangdidian, Xiaozishan/Pingxi Crags, Shengmu, Hehuan North), Expert/multi-day (Xueshan, Yushan, Dabajian, Qilai, Nanhu, Jiaming Lake, Beidawu).

## 3. Permits: national park entry, police mountain entry, lodge/cabin lotteries, lead times, official sites

### Takeaway
Two official portals matter. The first is the **Hike Smart Taiwan** portal (hike.taiwan.gov.tw), which covers police mountain-entry and links to the parks. The second is the **national park entry-permit system** (npm.cpami.gov.tw), with a separate **foreigner "advanced application" quota** that opens **4 months to 35 days** before the hike (Yushan, Xueshan, Dabajian). Normal domestic applications open 2 months to 5 days ahead. Jiaming Lake cabins are a Forestry agency lottery drawn 30 days ahead. Paiyun Lodge's foreigner quota is reduced to 18 per night during its renovation.

### Cited Findings
- Official portals: [Hike Smart Taiwan](https://hike.taiwan.gov.tw/en/web_index.aspx); [National Park entry-permit online application](https://npm.cpami.gov.tw/en/notice_1.aspx); [Taroko NP Mountain Permits page](https://www.taroko.gov.tw/en/cp.aspx?n=7973)
- **Yushan / Paiyun Lodge**: the foreigner "Paiyun Lodge Advanced Application" opens 4 months to 35 days before the hike date. It is a dedicated foreigner (passport/ARC) quota separate from the lottery, for Sunday–Thursday nights only. The foreigner quota was cut from 24 to 18 per weekday night from Sept 2025 while Paiyun Lodge is renovated, and the cut carried into 2026 — [Taiwan Obsessed Yushan permit guide](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/yushan-permit-application-guide/); [triptotip 2026 guide](https://www.triptotip.com/en/guides/yushan-hiking-permit-foreigners/)
- **Xueshan / 369 Cabin**: 369 Cabin is being rebuilt, and only a temporary campsite for 24 hikers is available. 369 accommodation is tied to the NP entry permit, so a successful permit guarantees a bunk/space. **Qika Cabin**: 24 beds via advanced reservation Thursday–Monday, not open on weekends or national holidays. Foreigners are advised to use the foreign quota and apply 4 months ahead — [unlockingtaiwan permits guide](https://unlockingtaiwan.com/taiwan-hiking-permits-guide/); [unlockingtaiwan Xueshan guide](https://unlockingtaiwan.com/xueshan-hiking-guide/); [iwannatravel FAQ](https://iwannatravel.com.sg/faq-for-xueshan)
- **Jiaming Lake cabin**: bed spaces are allocated by lottery, drawn at 3 p.m. thirty days before check-in. Each successive night must be won separately — [Jiaming Lake National Trail official (forest.gov.tw)](https://jmlnt-en.forest.gov.tw/room/camp_terms.php)
- **Dabajianshan**: national park permit required. Apply 2 months to 5 days before the hike, or 4 months to 35 days for the Foreign Advanced Application — [Taiwan Hikes Dabajian](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/100-peaks-dabajian-mountain.html)
- **Qilai Nanhua**: foreigners get a priority quota of 12 per day and apply 4 months to 2 months before the stay — [braveontw](https://www.braveontw.com/post/taiwan-hiking-permits-foreigners)
- **Nanhu / Qilai (Taroko NP)**: Nanhu capacity changes applied from 1 July 2025. From 28 April 2026 applications opened for two new Ecological Protection Area routes including "Qilai North/…" (snippet truncated) — [Taroko NP permits page / search summary](https://www.taroko.gov.tw/en/cp.aspx?n=7973)
- **Walami**: no permit needed to Shanfeng Waterfall and Jiaxin. Beyond that (Huangma, Walami Cabin) a permit is required — [Taiwan Hikes Walami](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/walami-trail.html)

### Inferences
- App rule of thumb: for any Shei-Pa / Yushan / Taroko high route, show "Apply 4 months → 35 days ahead (foreigner quota)" plus police mountain-entry via Hike Smart Taiwan. Hehuanshan day peaks, Taipei hikes, Sun Moon Lake, Alishan, Kenting and Walami-to-Jiaxin need no permit (inferred from sources describing them as permit-free day trails; see Taiwan Hikes "permit-free trails" list in search results).

## 4. Best season and hazards

### Takeaway
Northern coastal hikes are best October–April and should be avoided in typhoon/plum-rain season (May–September). High-mountain routes face winter snow and ice: Hehuanshan is Taiwan's main snow destination, and typhoons regularly wreck trails and trailheads (Qilai Nanhua trailhead, Mianyue Line tunnel, Walami).

### Cited Findings
- Caoling Historic Trail: best October–April when the east coast is dry and cool. Avoid May–September (typhoons, heavy rain, mud). Strong winds are common in open valley sections. A reviewer found the trail closed despite Google listing it as open — [Wanderlog Caoling](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/3781983/caoling-historic-trail); [takeyourbackpack Caoling](https://www.takeyourbackpack.com/backpacking-in-taiwan/hike-caoling-historic-trail/)
- Hehuanshan is marketed for sunrises, hiking and **snow** — [Taiwan Obsessed Hehuanshan guide](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/hehuanshan-sunrise-hiking-snow/)
- Typhoon damage examples: Qilai Nanhua original trailhead destroyed by a typhoon ([braveontw](https://www.braveontw.com/post/taiwan-hiking-permits-foreigners)). A tunnel on the Mianyue Line collapsed in an Aug 2025 typhoon ([Taiwan Obsessed Mianyue](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/mianyue-line-alishan/)). Walami was closed in autumn 2025 after typhoon damage ([Taiwan Obsessed closed list](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/)). Typhoon Halong (2025) has its own Wikipedia article ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Halong_(2025))); its specific trail impact was not confirmed.
- Huangdidian and Xiaozishan: exposed ridges with chains/ropes, so slipping is the main risk — [Foreigners in Taiwan](https://www.foreignersintaiwan.com/blog/huangdidian-trail); [AllTrails Xiaozishan](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/taiwan/new-taipei-city/xiaozishan-trail)

### Inferences
- Display a typhoon-season banner (roughly June–October) on all hikes, and a "check closure before going" flag on Taroko, Alishan, Walami, Sun Moon Lake and Yilan trails, given how many 2025–26 closures came from rain or typhoon damage.

### Gaps
- Month-by-month best-season data for the high peaks (e.g., winter crampon/permit restrictions on Xueshan and Yushan, and whether NPs require snow gear Dec–Mar) was not sourced this session.

## 5. Public transport access

### Takeaway
Most Taipei-area hikes are reachable by MRT, TRA train or city/intercity bus: Elephant Mtn (MRT Xiangshan Exit 2), Jinmianshan (MRT Xihu), Qixingshan (bus 1717/108), Teapot Mtn (bus 1062 to Jinguashi), Bitou Cape (bus 1811), and the Pingxi line for Sandiaoling and Pingxi Crags. High-mountain trailheads have only sparse bus links, e.g. Hehuanshan via Nantou bus 6664 then 6658A from Cingjing, with 3 buses a day.

### Cited Findings
- **Elephant Mountain**: MRT Red Line to Xiangshan Station, Exit 2, then a ~10 min walk south to the trailhead — [Taipei Tourism Guide / search summary](https://www.taipeitourism.org/elephant-mountain-taipei-hike/)
- **Qixingshan**: MRT Red Line to Shilin Station Exit 2, walk to the Shilin Official Residence stop, then **bus 1717** to Xiaoyoukeng Visitor Center. Hike over Qixingshan to Lengshuikeng. **Bus 108** (park loop) links Xiaoyoukeng, Lengshuikeng and Qingtiangang, and 108 or 1717 run back down — [Taiwan Obsessed: Taipei to Yangmingshan](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/taipei-to-yangmingshan/)
- **Jinmianshan**: ~15 min walk from **Xihu Station (MRT Brown/Wenhu Line)** — [Medium/brownbeat](https://brownbeat.medium.com/taiwan-travels-jinmianshan-hiking-trail-a7d059ced638)
- **Teapot Mountain**: **bus 1062** from Taipei (board at Zhongxiao Fuxing MRT) direct to Jiufen and Jinguashi; get off at Quanji Temple. Other buses in the area include **788, 856, 965** — [The Sandy Feet](https://thesandyfeet.com/teapot-mountain-hike-taiwan-jiufen/); [takeyourbackpack Teapot](https://www.takeyourbackpack.com/backpacking-in-taiwan/hike-teapot-mountain-trail/)
- **Bitou Cape**: Kuo-Kuang (Guo-Kuang) **bus 1811** from Taipei Main Station (~1 h 18 min), or bus **4162** then **1812** — [search summary of Taipei hikes sources, e.g. Embrace Horizons](https://embracehorizons.com/best-hikes-in-taipei-transit/)
- **Sandiaoling**: the trail starts alongside the Pingxi railway line (implying TRA Sandiaoling Station) — [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/taiwan/new-taipei-city/ruifang/waterfall)
- **Xizhi Dajianshan**: trailhead near Tianxiu Temple, reachable by public transport or a walk from Xizhi Station — [mindtrip / taiwantravelblog](https://taiwantravelblog.com/xizhi-dajianshan/)
- **Hehuanshan**: no direct bus from Taichung. Nantou bus **6664** leaves Taichung Gancheng Station 08:30 and Taichung HSR (B1) 08:50 toward Puli/Cingjing. From Cingjing Farm, bus **6658A** is the only bus to Wuling / Songxue Lodge / Xiaofengkou, with 3 buses a day. ## 6. Current status as of 2025–2026 (closures/reopenings)

### Takeaway
**Taroko's signature gorge trails, Shakadang, Baiyang, Swallow Grotto and Zhuilu, remain CLOSED**, and Shakadang, Swallow Grotto and Baiyang are not expected to be repaired until **2031**. Dali–Datong, the Taroko Terrace Trail, Lushui (a short section only, since Feb 2026) and the Liwushan/Cianyanlishan trails are open. Elsewhere, closures in 2026 include **Alishan's Tashan (upper) and Mianyue Line** (July 2026–June 2027, though Mianyue was reported accessible again from 1 Sept 2026 via alternative access), **Sun Moon Lake's Shuishe Dashan** (closed since Jan 2026), and the **Holy Mother/Matcha Mountain trail** (closed June–Nov 2026). **Walami reopened 11 Aug 2026.**

### Cited Findings
**Taroko National Park (after the 3 April 2024 M7.2 earthquake):**
- Reopened: visitor center, Taroko Terrace Trail, Tiansiang Recreation Area, Dacingshui Recreation Area, Chongde Trail, **Dali–Datong Trail**, **Liwushan** and **Cianyanlishan** hiking trails. Buluowan Terrace and Tunnel of Nine Turns were to reopen later in 2026 (as of March 2026) — [Taipei Times 6 Mar 2026](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/03/06/2003853379)
- **Shakadang, Swallow Grotto, Baiyang: not repaired until 2031.** Zhuilu Old Road remains closed, with any reopening date to be announced — [Taipei Times 6 Mar 2026](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/03/06/2003853379); [Taiwan Obsessed: Is Taroko open](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/is-taroko-gorge-open/)
- **Lushui**: partially reopened Feb 2026. The first section of the Lushui Trail (to the small suspension bridge, ~250 m) and the start of the Lushui–Wenshan Trail (~450 m) are accessible — [search summary of 2026 Taroko status pages, e.g. explorertom](https://explorertom.com/en/taroko-national-park-guide/)
- CONFLICT: Taiwan Obsessed's mid-2026 closed list says "all hiking trails are closed in Taroko Gorge except one 4–5 h hike that leaves from the park entrance but does not follow the gorge" (likely Dali–Datong or the Taroko Terrace/Liwushan area) — [Taiwan Obsessed closed list](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/). This contradicts the [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/03/06/2003853379) list of several reopened trails. The difference is probably in what counts as a "gorge hiking trail" vs. a recreation-area walk. Check the official Taroko NP site before publishing.

**Alishan:**
- Tashan (upper portions) and Mianyue Line are closed 1 July 2026 – end of June 2027 for trail repairs. Mianyue Line is reported "accessible again as of 1 Sept 2026, with alternative access routes" — [Taiwan Obsessed closed list](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/)
- Earlier: one of the first Mianyue Line tunnels collapsed in an Aug 2025 typhoon, and the trail was closed through at least 30 Sept 2025 — [Taiwan Obsessed Mianyue Line](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/mianyue-line-alishan/)

- CONFLICT on end date: Taiwan Obsessed lists "Matcha Mountain Trail closed for repairs until **26 Nov 2026**" and "road to the St. Maria Trail trailhead under construction 10 June – **10 Nov 2026**" — [Taiwan Obsessed closed list](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/).
