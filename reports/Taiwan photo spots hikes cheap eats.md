# Chase Taiwan's lanterns, peaks and NT$50 bowls

A first-time visitor can cover Taiwan's best photos, hikes and cheap food on public transport and a small budget, as long as they plan around a few hard constraints. The photos that matter most are the Taipei 101 skyline from **Elephant Mountain at dusk**, **Jiufen's lantern stairs at blue hour**, Yehliu's eroding **Queen's Head**, **Sun Moon Lake**, the **Alishan sunrise from Zhushan**, the **Gaomei Wetlands at sunset**, **Mr. Brown Avenue**, Penghu's **Twin-Heart Stone Weir** and the reopened **Chongde viewpoint at Qingshui Cliff**. The best day hikes need only a transit card: Elephant Mountain, Qixingshan, Bitou Cape, Teapot Mountain, Caoling (October to April), Hehuanshan's 3,422 m main peak (no permit needed) and Sun Moon Lake's Maolan Trail. Yushan and Xueshan need a national-park permit, applied for **4 months to 35 days ahead** under the foreigner quota, and Paiyun Lodge's foreigner quota is down to **18 per night** while the lodge is renovated. Cheap food is cheap almost everywhere. Bafang dumplings cost about **NT$6–10 each**, braised pork rice **NT$30–50**, a convenience-store tea egg **NT$13–15**, and Tainan beef soup is a dawn ritual. Several famous names should not appear in the app as recommendations at all: Taroko's **Shakadang, Swallow Grotto and Baiyang** trails (not expected to be repaired before **2031**), **Zhuilu** (no date) and **Changchun Shrine** (2029); the **Holy Mother / Matcha Mountain** trail (closed until at least mid-to-late November 2026); **Shuishe Dashan**; Alishan's **Tashan and Mianyue Line** (until mid-2027); **Sanxiantai's dragon bridge** (until end-2027); and the brown-sugar milk shop **Chen San Ding**, which has closed. One caveat covers the whole dataset. Wikipedia, Wikidata and OpenStreetMap were blocked during research, so **apart from three summit coordinates, every lat/lng below is an approximate placeholder (~)**, and every price should be read as "last reported".

## Every coordinate is a placeholder until geocoded

The research network blocked Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, Wikidata, OpenStreetMap, the Taroko National Park site, Taipei Times, Focus Taiwan, Michelin and several large travel blogs. Most facts below therefore come from search-result snippets of the cited pages, not from full reads. The coordinates for photo spots and central and southern food shops come from the researchers' own knowledge, rounded to about three decimals (roughly 100–500 m accuracy, and worse where marked "low"). The northern food notes deliberately gave no coordinates and asked that the street addresses be geocoded instead. Only the **Yushan, Hehuanshan and Qixingshan** summit coordinates come from Wikipedia (via search) ([Wikipedia: Yu Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_Shan); [Wikipedia: Hehuanshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hehuanshan); [Wikipedia: Qixing Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixing_Mountain_%28Taipei%29)). They are summits, not trailheads. Photo links are uneven too. About a dozen specific Wikimedia Commons file pages appeared as live search hits but were never opened, so their licences and framing are unchecked. Everything else links to a Commons category, an English Wikipedia article or an official tourism page. Before launch, the app should run a batch pull from Wikidata/Commons (coordinates plus `prop=imageinfo` for thumbnails and licences).

Taiwan street-food prices rose broadly over 2023–2026, so older blog prices are probably 10–20% low.

## Northern Taiwan packs 30-plus shots within two hours of Taipei

The north is where a first-timer gets the most photos per hour of travel, and timing matters more than location. **Elephant Mountain** is a 184 m hill directly behind Xinyi. The Six Giant Rocks viewpoint is about 20 minutes up from **MRT Xiangshan Exit 2**, and the best window is the 30–45 minutes when Taipei 101's lights come on: about **18:00–18:45 in summer**, while sunset falls around **17:15 in December** ([taipeitourism.org](https://www.taipeitourism.org/elephant-mountain-taipei-hike/); [taipei-escape.com](https://www.taipei-escape.com/guides/elephant-mountain-hike/)). **Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall** is free, its grounds are open 05:00–24:00, and since 2024 the changing of the guard takes place **outdoors on Liberty Square, hourly 09:00–17:00** ([taipeitourism.org](https://www.taipeitourism.org/chiang-kai-shek-memorial-hall/)). Yangmingshan has two seasonal peaks. The Zhuzihu calla lilies peak from **mid-March to mid-April**, and the fields grow 80–90% of Taiwan's callas ([travel.taipei](https://www.travel.taipei/en/pictorial/article/57703)). Silvergrass at Qingtiangang and Qixingshan runs **October to December, peaking in November** ([Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/qingtiangang-grassland-taipei/)).

East of Taipei, **bus 1062 from Zhongxiao Fuxing** goes straight to Jiufen and Jinguashi, and bus 965 runs from Beimen ([Nick Kembel](https://www.nickkembel.com/taipei-to-jiufen-to-shifen/)). The **Gold Museum** charges **NT$80** and closes on the **first Monday of each month** ([Gold Museum](https://www.gep-en.ntpc.gov.tw/xmdoc/cont?xsmsid=0G274606722488077919)). **Yehliu's Queen's Head** is still standing, but its neck has thinned from about 220 cm in the 1990s to **116.59 cm (October 2024)**. Reinforcement has cut weathering on treated rocks to about a tenth of the untreated rate ([Taiwan Merch](https://taiwanmerch.co/culture/yehliu-geopark-taiwan-guide/)). The ticketed night tours that light up the rock ran **28 June to 12 July 2026, 18:30–21:00** ([Tourism Administration](https://eng.taiwan.net.tw/m1.aspx?sNo=0002019&lid=081749)). Among the hidden gems, sources back **Wangyou Valley** (a 1.7 km easy trail with a white staircase above the sea, reached by Keelung bus 103) ([Trip.com](https://www.trip.com/moments/poi-wangyougu-13505943/)), **Laomei Green Reef** (algae-covered troughs at their greenest in April at low tide) ([hoponworld](https://hoponworld.com/laomei-green-reef/)), **Bitou Cape** (a 3.5 km loop and the north coast's favourite drone shot) ([mstravelsolo](https://www.mstravelsolo.com/bitoujiao-trail/)) and Kris Yao's **Lanyang Museum** (NT$100, **closed Wednesdays**) ([Lanyang Museum FAQ](https://www.lym.gov.tw/en/audience-service/faq/)). The research found no supporting detail for Zhishanyan, Mingchi, Smangus, Neiwan, Shitoushan, Shengxing/Longteng Bridge or Heping Island, so their rows carry the DETAILS-U flag.

The Maokong Gondola closes for several weeks each year for maintenance. In 2026 it was suspended on **20–24 April and 8–28 June** ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/06/02/2003858415)). Taipingshan was partly closed from **3 March to 30 April 2026**, and its Jianqing Huaigu Trail now ends after about 900 m because of typhoon damage ([KKday listing](https://www.kkday.com/en/product/100467-yilan-taipingshan-national-forest-recreation-area-admission-ticket-taiwan); [Taiwan Hikes](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/jancing-trail-taipingshan.html)). AllTrails users reported the Sandiaoling Waterfall Trail closed in May 2026, so it appears only in the closures table.

## Central, south and east trade convenience for scale

Away from Taipei, the photos get bigger and the logistics get harder, and two disasters still shape the east. In Taichung, the **Gaomei Wetlands** turbine-and-boardwalk sunset is the signature shot, and Commons holds a dedicated sunset file ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wind_power-Kaumei.jpg)). **Rainbow Village** is open (Tue–Sun 09:00–17:00, free), but workers painted over about half of Huang Yung-fu's murals in 2022, so much of what visitors now see is a repaint ([Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/rainbow-village-taichung/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/travel/article/rainbow-village-taiwan-intl-hnk/index.html)). For **Sun Moon Lake**, Tourist Shuttle **6670** leaves Taichung Gancheng Station about every 30 minutes between 07:20 and 19:45 and takes roughly 2 hours ([Taiwanderers](https://taiwanderers.com/how-to-get-to-sun-moon-lake/)). **Alishan** is fully reachable by rail again. The Alishan Forest Railway's Shizilu–Alishan section reopened on **6 July 2024** after 15 years, and the trip takes about 5 hours, with a 30-minute stop at Fenqihu ([Foreigners in Taiwan](https://www.foreignersintaiwan.com/blog/alishan-forest-railway)). The Zhushan sunrise train costs **NT$150 one way**, and tickets must be bought the day before ([Nick Kembel](https://www.nickkembel.com/alishan-taiwan-sunrise/)). Hehuanshan has a Dark Sky Park and the 3,275 m Wuling Pass, but only **three 6658A buses a day** run up from Cingjing ([Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/hehuanshan-sunrise-hiking-snow/)).

In Tainan and Kaohsiung, opening hours and tides decide the shot. The **Jingzaijiao tile-paved salt fields** (free, 09:00–18:00) are best at sunset, but bus 61 runs only twice a day, Friday to Monday ([twtainan.net](https://www.twtainan.net/en/attractions/detail/4642); [Nick Kembel](https://www.nickkembel.com/qigu-salt-mountain-tainan-jingzijiao-wapan-salt-fields-taiwan/)). The **Sicao Green Tunnel** mangrove boat costs **NT$200** and runs 08:00–16:00 ([twtainan.net](https://www.twtainan.net/en/attractions/detail/4506)). **Fo Guang Shan Buddha Museum** is free and **closed Tuesdays** ([Tourism Administration](https://eng.taiwan.net.tw/m1.aspx?sNo=0002121&id=A12-00163)). The status of Kaohsiung's **Dragon and Tiger Pagodas** is genuinely unclear. They reopened in March or April 2025, but a December 2025 blog post reports them closed ([Trip.com](https://us.trip.com/travel-guide/attraction/kaohsiung/dragon-and-tiger-pagodas-92979/); [blog](https://cavinteo.blogspot.com/2025/12/dragon-and-tiger-pagodas-closed-kaohsiung-city.html)).

On the east coast, **Mr. Brown Avenue** in Chishang has no utility poles. The rice is golden in **late May–June and late October–November**, and the best light is **05:30–07:30**, before the tour groups arrive ([we123go](https://we123go.com/article/taiwan-taitung-mrbrownnavenue-a2026guidetotaiwansmosticonicricefields)). The **Chongde viewpoint above Qingshui Cliff** reopened on **1 July 2025**, but the Huide viewpoint is being rebuilt ([Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/)). **Sanxiantai's eight-arch bridge and trail are closed from May 2026 to the end of 2027**, although the recreation area itself is open (same source). Further inland, the Matai'an barrier lake overtopped on **23 September 2025**, sending about 60 million tonnes of water and debris into Guangfu. At least 19 people died, and Guangfu station was flooded ([Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202509230029); [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/front/archives/2025/09/24/2003844324)). The app should therefore warn about rail and road links through central Hualien. Offshore, Penghu's **Twin-Heart Stone Weir** (about 700 years old) shows its full double heart only as the tide falls ([PENGHU-GO](https://www.penghu-go.tw/penghugo/index.php?action=brands_detail&br_uid=2078)). **Matsu's Blue Tears** peak from April to June, best in May, and show best after midnight on dark, calm nights ([Occasional Traveller](https://theoccasionaltraveller.com/blue-tears-matsu-islands-taiwan/)). Penghu's 2026 fireworks festival ran from **4 May to 25 August** ([PENGHU-GO](https://www.penghu-go.tw/penghugo/index.php?action=news_detail&br_uid=2897&web_lang=en-us)).

## Day hikes need only a transit card; 3,000-metre peaks need four months' notice

Taiwan's hiking splits into two very different products. The first is short, transit-reachable day hikes. **Elephant Mountain** takes 1.5–2 hours round trip from the MRT ([taipeitourism.org](https://www.taipeitourism.org/elephant-mountain-taipei-hike/)). **Qixingshan**, Taipei's highest peak at 1,120 m, is a 2–3 hour traverse from Xiaoyoukeng to Lengshuikeng, starting with bus 1717 from MRT Shilin Exit 2 and returning on the 108 loop ([Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/taipei-to-yangmingshan/)). **Jinmianshan** is a 30–40 minute climb from MRT Xihu ([Medium](https://brownbeat.medium.com/taiwan-travels-jinmianshan-hiking-trail-a7d059ced638)). **Teapot Mountain** starts from the Quanji Temple stop on bus 1062 ([The Sandy Feet](https://thesandyfeet.com/teapot-mountain-hike-taiwan-jiufen/)), and **Bitou Cape** is about 1 h 18 min from Taipei Main Station on Kuo-Kuang bus 1811 ([Embrace Horizons](https://embracehorizons.com/best-hikes-in-taipei-transit/)). Two classics come with real exposure. **Huangdidian** and the **Pingxi Crags/Xiaozishan** both cross narrow rock spines on iron rungs, chains and ropes ([Foreigners in Taiwan](https://www.foreignersintaiwan.com/blog/huangdidian-trail); [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/taiwan/new-taipei-city/xiaozishan-trail)), so the app should tag them "hard/exposed". The **Caoling Historic Trail** is best from **October to April**. Avoid it from May to September because of typhoons and mud ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/3781983/caoling-historic-trail)). In central Taiwan, Sun Moon Lake's **Maolan Trail** (3 km of tea fields, open 09:00–17:00) has replaced the closed Shuishe Dashan as the lake's featured hike ([Sun Moon Lake NSA](https://www.sunmoonlake.gov.tw/en/attractions/detail/147)). **Hehuanshan's main peak (3,422 m)** gives a first-timer a 3,000 m summit with no permit, although the trailhead depends on the three-a-day 6658A bus ([Taiwan Hikes](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/100-peaks-hehuanshan.html)).

The second product is the permit peaks, where planning means booking months ahead. Foreigners can apply through a dedicated **"advanced application" quota 4 months to 35 days before the hike** for Yushan, Xueshan and Dabajian, while the normal window is 2 months to 5 days ([Taiwan Hikes](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/100-peaks-dabajian-mountain.html); [National Park permit system](https://npm.cpami.gov.tw/en/notice_1.aspx)). At **Yushan (3,952 m)**, Paiyun Lodge's foreigner quota runs Sunday to Thursday nights only and has been **cut from 24 to 18 per night** since September 2025 while the lodge is renovated ([triptotip](https://www.triptotip.com/en/guides/yushan-hiking-permit-foreigners/)). At **Xueshan (3,886 m)**, the 369 Cabin is being rebuilt, and a temporary campsite for 24 hikers stands in for it ([unlockingtaiwan](https://unlockingtaiwan.com/taiwan-hiking-permits-guide/)). **Jiaming Lake** cabins go by lottery, drawn at **15:00 thirty days before check-in**, and each night must be won separately ([Forestry agency](https://jmlnt-en.forest.gov.tw/room/camp_terms.php)). **Qilai Nanhua** offers foreigners 12 priority places a day, but a typhoon destroyed its original trailhead, adding 3 km each way ([braveontw](https://www.braveontw.com/post/taiwan-hiking-permits-foreigners)). Hikers may no longer climb **Dabajian's spire**; they stop at its base ([Taiwan Hikes](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/100-peaks-dabajian-mountain.html)). **Walami**, in Yushan National Park's east, reopened on **11 August 2026** after repeated typhoon closures. Without a permit, hikers can go as far as Shanfeng Waterfall (1.7 km) and Jiaxin (4.5 km) ([Taiwan Hikes](https://www.taiwanhikes.com/blog-posts/walami-trail.html); [Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/)).

Reports on Taroko disagree, and the app should say so. The Taipei Times (March 2026) lists **Dali–Datong, Taroko Terrace, Chongde, Liwushan and Cianyanlishan** as reopened, with **Lushui** partly open since February 2026 (the first ~250 m, plus ~450 m of the Lushui–Wenshan Trail). A mid-2026 blog says only one 4–5 hour hike from the park entrance is open ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/03/06/2003853379); [Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/)). The difference probably comes down to what counts as a "gorge" trail. The Taroko row should be PARTIAL, with a link to the official park site.

## Bafang-style chains and convenience stores feed you for under NT$150

The cheapest reliable meals come from chains that are on almost every block, and the app should treat them as "always available" fallbacks. **Bafang Yunji** was founded in 1998 and has about **777 stores**. Its potstickers usually cost **NT$6–10 each**, so a 10-piece order is plausibly NT$60–90 today, an inference rather than a sourced price ([Taiwanderers](https://taiwanderers.com/taiwan-chain-restaurants-cafes/)). **Formosa Chang** began as a Taipei stall in 1960 and built a national chain on braised pork rice ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formosa_Chang)). The giant fried chicken cutlet costs **NT$90–95 at Hot-Star's Shilin original as of June 2026** ([TaiwanMerch](https://taiwanmerch.co/culture/shilin-night-market-taiwan-guide/)). Two Peck's NT$60–65 figure comes from posts written around 2008–2010 and is surely out of date ([Alvinology](https://alvinology.com/2008/10/03/two-peck-fried-chicken/)). Mos Burger's rice-burger sets run **NT$180–220** on aggregator menus ([everymenuprices](https://everymenuprices.com/mos-burger-menu-with-prices/)). Breakfast means the "Western-style" chains **Mei Er Mei** and **My Warm Day**; My Warm Day alone has over 1,000 shops ([Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/taiwanese-breakfast/)).

Convenience stores are a food destination in their own right. 7-Eleven has about **7,021 stores** and FamilyMart about **4,251**. Tea eggs cost **NT$13–15** (FamilyMart's price rose to NT$13 in December 2022), roasted sweet potatoes **NT$30–60** by weight, oden from about **NT$20 a piece**, and FamilyMart soft serve about **NT$50** ([Taipei Tourism](https://www.taipeitourism.org/taiwan-convenience-stores/); [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202212190023)). One source prices FamilyMart's Famichiki at **NT$99**, which looks high, so that figure is flagged. **Papaya milk** dates from the 1970s, and the common bottled version is made by Uni-President ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya_milk)). The famous fresh version is at **Kaohsiung Milk King** (since 1966, open 07:00–01:00), where the in-store price is disputed: one menu lists NT$60/35, and UberEats shows NT$100 ([nigi33](https://nigi33.tw/khmilk-king/); [fonfood](https://www.fonfood.com/store/977842)). Two Taiwanese teahouses claim to have invented bubble tea: Chun Shui Tang (1987) and Hanlin (1986). In 2019 a court called the question irrelevant, because bubble tea cannot be patented ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2018/11/13/2003704115); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_tea)). For brown-sugar milk, the app should send users to **Xing Fu Tang, Tiger Sugar or The Alley**, not to **Chen San Ding**, which Yelp and Foursquare list as closed ([Yelp](https://m.yelp.com/biz/%E9%99%B3%E4%B8%89%E9%BC%8E%E9%9D%92%E8%9B%99%E9%AE%AE%E5%A5%B6-%E4%B8%AD%E6%AD%A3%E5%8D%80)).

## Famous shops by city: dawn beef soup to NT$7 baozi

City shops are where the app earns its keep, and two patterns stand out. First, many of the most famous cheap shops are **Michelin Bib Gourmand** night-market stalls. The 2025 Taiwan list had **144** Bibs (37 in Taipei). The 2026 list, announced in July 2026, has **146**, with Taichung at 23, Tainan 30 and Kaohsiung 21 ([Michelin 2025](https://guide.michelin.com/tw/en/article/michelin-guide-ceremony/michelin-guide-taiwan-2025-bib-gourmand-selection); [Focus Taiwan 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202607140017)). Several tourist favourites are **not** Bibs: Wenzhang, Liu Qian and A-Cun beef soup, Du Hsiao Yueh, and A-Tang congee. Kaohsiung's count fell from 24 to 21, so tag each shop "Bib (2025); check 2026" unless it has been confirmed. Second, **opening hours are the binding constraint**. Tainan has a dawn window and a late window. Liu Qian Beef Soup opens only **Friday to Monday, 05:00–11:00**, Wenzhang is **closed Thursdays**, and the Garden Night Market runs only **Thursday to Sunday**, so a visitor in Tainan on a Tuesday or Wednesday misses Liu Qian and the Garden Night Market, and a Thursday visitor misses both Liu Qian and Wenzhang ([rabbitfunaround](https://rabbitfunaround.com/blog/post/6000beefsoup); [travel.yam](https://travel.yam.com/article/138067)).

Taipei's essentials are cheap and well documented. **Fuhang Soy Milk** (MRT Shandao Temple Exit 5) is closed Mondays and best at 06:00–08:00 ([Eating in Taipei](https://eatingintaipei.com/fuhang-soy-milk/)). **Jin Feng** braised pork rice (MRT CKS Memorial Hall Exit 2) is open until 01:00 ([Trip.com](https://www.trip.com/moments/poi-jin-feng-braised-pork-rice-10355109/)), and **Liu Yu Zi** fried taro balls at Ningxia cost NT$25–30 and are closed Thursdays ([Daniel Food Diary](https://danielfooddiary.com/2018/03/07/liuyuzai/)). Outside Taipei, three prices show how cheap the floor still is: Lukang's **Wang Wang vermicelli paste at NT$35 (2026)**, **Gongzheng Baozi in Hualien at NT$7 a bun** after moving to 46 Ren'ai St, and Kaohsiung's Bib-listed **Cianjin Braised Pork Rice at NT$40** ([bunnyann](https://bunnyann.tw/gonoodles2026/); [vocus](https://vocus.cc/article/6791b849fd897800019e2a0a); [nigi33](https://nigi33.tw/qian-jin-rice/)). Not everything is cheap. Tainan's A-Tang milkfish congee now costs **close to NT$300** with milkfish belly, so budget users should try Bib-listed A-Hsing or Wang's Fish Skin instead ([momoblog](https://momoblog.tw/atang/)).

## Closed and partly closed: keep these out of recommendations

The closures fall into three types, and the app should handle each one differently. Earthquake damage at **Taroko** is long-term. Shakadang, Swallow Grotto and Baiyang will not be repaired until **2031**, and Changchun Shrine until **2029** ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/03/06/2003853379)). These should be hard CLOSED records that point users to the open alternatives. **Typhoon and rain closures with set end dates** (Holy Mother/Matcha Mountain, Alishan's Tashan and Mianyue, Sanxiantai's bridge) should be date-bounded records that switch on automatically. **Uncertain or crowd-reported closures** (Sandiaoling, the Dragon and Tiger Pagodas, Buluowan, the Xiaowulai Skywalk) should show a "check before you go" banner rather than be hidden. The Matcha Mountain row shows why: two sources give different end dates, **6 November** and **26 November 2026**, so the safe wording is "closed until at least mid-to-late November 2026" ([unlockingtaiwan](https://unlockingtaiwan.com/matcha-mountain-yilan/); [Taiwan Obsessed](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/closed-attractions/)).

## Conclusion

The real constraint on a first-timer is not budget but timing. A NT$50 bowl, a free viewpoint and a permit-free 3,422 m summit are all within reach, provided the visitor arrives on an opening day, at the right tide or light, and outside a closure window. That makes the app's scheduling logic (closed days, dawn-only shops, seasonal windows and date-bounded closures) more valuable than any single listing. Tainan shows it best: whether a visitor gets the city's two most famous beef soups depends entirely on the day of the week.

The dataset is strongest on status, transport, hours and prices, and weakest on geography and imagery. Coordinates and photo licences are therefore the first engineering task before launch: one Wikidata/OSM geocoding pass and one Commons API pass would turn about 200 approximate rows into shippable ones. The second task is a refresh routine. The 2025–26 record of typhoons, an earthquake and a barrier-lake flood shows that Taiwan's trails and east coast change status several times a year, so every row should carry a last-verified date, and the "OPEN-PRESUMED" rows should be re-checked first.
